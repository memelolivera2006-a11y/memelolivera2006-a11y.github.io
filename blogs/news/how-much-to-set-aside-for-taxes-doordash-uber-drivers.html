How Much to Set Aside for Taxes as a DoorDash or Uber Driver (2026)

Short answer: set aside a share of your profit, not your gross: about 14% if driving is your only income and your profit stays under the standard deduction, about 26% if you drive on top of a job in the 12% bracket, and about 36% in the 22% bracket, plus your state income tax. Profit is pay and tips minus your mileage deduction and other expenses.

Skip the math: the Gig Driver Mileage & Expense Log ($17, Excel + Google Sheets) does it for you: log pay, tips and miles per trip, get the right 2026 IRS rate and your quarterly tax set-aside.

Where the percentages come from

  • Self-employment tax: 15.3% on 92.35% of profit, which is 14.13% of profit. Everyone with $400+ of profit pays it.
  • Federal income tax: your top bracket on the profit (10%, 12%, 22% and up). If your total income stays under the standard deduction, this part is $0.
  • State income tax: depends on your state; some have none.

The real income tax ends up a bit lower than the bracket rate, because half of your self-employment tax and the qualified business income deduction come off first. Treat the percentages as a safe ceiling.

Setting aside 26% of the full $800 would park $208 a week, $78.40 more than needed. Setting aside nothing means a bill of about $6,700 if every week looks like this one. The mileage log is what turns $800 into $496 of taxable profit.

Make it automatic

  • Open a separate savings account just for taxes.
  • Every payout, move your percentage of that week's profit into it, not of the payout.
  • Pay from it on the estimated tax dates: the 2026 quarterly schedule, with the last one due January 15, 2027.
  • Delivery and rideshare tips may also qualify for the no tax on tips deduction, which lowers the income tax part.
