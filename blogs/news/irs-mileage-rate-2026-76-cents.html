IRS Mileage Rate 2026: 76 Cents From July 1 (72.5 Before)

Short answer: the IRS business mileage rate for 2026 is 72.5 cents a mile for January 1 to June 30 and 76 cents a mile from July 1. The IRS raised it mid-year because fuel prices went up. Miles driven before July 1 still use 72.5 cents - you do not get 76 cents on the whole year.

The three mistakes that cost people the most

  1. Using one rate for the whole year. 76 cents on January's miles overstates the deduction; 72.5 cents on December's miles leaves money behind.
  2. Counting the commute. Driving from home to a regular workplace is commuting, not business. For gig work, the business miles are the ones driven for the gig - check IRS Topic 510 for how your situation counts.
  3. No written log. The IRS wants the date, the miles and the business purpose, written at or near the time. Odometer guesses in April do not hold up.

Standard rate or actual costs?

You can deduct the standard rate or your actual car costs (gas, repairs, insurance, depreciation) for the business share - not both. If you want the standard rate for a car you own, you generally have to choose it in the first year you use that car for business.
