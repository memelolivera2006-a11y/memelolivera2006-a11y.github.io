Cost of Goods Sold for Resellers: Schedule C Part III, Worked Out

Short answer: cost of goods sold (COGS) is what you paid for the items you actually sold this year, not everything you bought. The formula on Schedule C, Part III is: inventory at the start of the year + purchases (minus anything you kept for yourself) + other costs such as freight-in − inventory at the end of the year. The result goes on line 4 and comes straight off your sales before profit, and self-employment tax, are figured.

The lines, one by one

Schedule C line What a reseller puts there
35 Inventory at beginning of year What you paid for unsold items on hand January 1. It should match last year's ending inventory.
36 Purchases less items withdrawn for personal use Everything you bought to resell this year, at the price you actually paid, minus the cost of anything you pulled out to keep.
37 Cost of labor Usually nothing for a small reseller. Don't include anything paid to yourself.
38 Materials and supplies Mostly for makers. Mailers and tape for shipping are usually expenses.
39 Other costs Freight-in: shipping you paid to get items you bought for resale to you, such as a bulk lot or a pallet.
41 Inventory at end of year What you paid for every item still unsold on December 31.
42 Cost of goods sold Line 40 (35 through 39 added) minus line 41. Also goes on line 4.

A worked example

A second-year eBay and Poshmark seller. On January 1, 2026 they had unsold items that cost $1,450. During 2026 they bought $4,800 of stock, kept a $120 jacket for themselves, and paid $180 shipping on two bulk lots. On December 31 the unsold pile cost $1,900.

Amount
Line 35: beginning inventory $1,450.00
Line 36: purchases $4,800 minus $120 kept $4,680.00
Line 39: freight-in $180.00
Line 40: goods available for sale $6,310.00
Line 41: ending inventory − $1,900.00
Line 42: cost of goods sold $4,410.00

Now the rest of Schedule C:

Amount
Sales $11,200.00
Returns and refunds − $300.00
Cost of goods sold − $4,410.00
Gross profit $6,490.00
Platform fees − $1,430.00
Shipping labels to buyers − $1,150.00
Supplies (mailers, tape, labels) − $160.00
Net profit $3,750.00
Self-employment tax (15.3% of 92.35%) $529.86

The three mistakes that cost resellers money

1. Deducting everything you bought. It feels natural to write off every haul, but you deduct what you sold. Here, this year's purchases plus freight are $4,860.00, while true COGS is $4,410.00. Deducting everything would overstate COGS by $450.00, exactly the growth in the unsold pile ($1,900.00 at year end against $1,450.00 at the start), and understate profit by the same amount. A big December haul is next year's deduction, when it sells.

2. Forgetting last year's leftovers. If this seller skipped line 35, COGS would drop to $2,960.00 and they would pay self-employment tax on $1,450.00 of profit they never made: about $204.88 extra, before income tax. Items you bought in 2025 and sold in 2026 are a 2026 deduction.

3. Not knowing what things cost. Thrift, garage sale and estate finds rarely come with receipts. Write the price down the day you buy, ideally per item. For a bulk lot, keep the lot's total cost and a list of what was in it, so the cost follows the items as they sell.

First year, returns and leftovers

First year selling? Line 35 is usually zero: you start with no stock bought for resale. Your first ending inventory then becomes next year's line 35, so the count you do this December matters for two tax years.

Buyer returns: refunds go on Schedule C line 2 (returns and allowances), which reduces sales. If the item comes back to you and is still sellable, it is unsold stock again, and belongs in your year-end count at what you paid for it.

Items you gave up on: if something will never sell, keep a note of it. Pub. 538 covers how inventory is valued; ask a tax professional before writing stock down.

Do small sellers have to count inventory?

Not always in the formal sense. Per IRS Publication 334, a small business taxpayer (average annual gross receipts of $31 million or less for the 3 prior years, indexed for inflation, and not a tax shelter) can choose not to keep an inventory. One option is to treat inventory as non-incidental materials and supplies: the cost is deducted in the year you provide the item to your customer. The other is to follow the method used in your own books and records. For a reseller, the first option lands in the same place as the formula above: you deduct items when they sell. You still need to know what each one cost. Changing methods later generally means filing Form 3115.

What does not go in COGS

  • Platform fees and shipping labels to buyers: business expenses in Part II, not Part III.
  • Miles driven to thrift stores and the post office: a car expense, at 72.5 cents a mile through June 30, 2026 and 76 cents from July 1 if you use the standard rate.
  • Items you kept: take them out of purchases (line 36), as in the example.
  • Donated inventory: Pub. 334 has a special rule; any contribution deduction is the smaller of fair market value or your cost, it comes out of opening inventory, and it is not part of COGS.

Your year-end checklist

  1. On December 31, list every unsold item with what you paid. That total is line 41, and next year's line 35.
  2. Total this year's purchases from your log, minus anything you kept.
  3. Add freight-in on stock you bought.
  4. Download each platform's annual sales and fee report in January.

FAQ

Can I deduct inventory I haven't sold yet?

No. Unsold items at year end are your ending inventory; you deduct their cost in the year they sell.

Is shipping part of cost of goods sold?

Shipping you pay to get stock to you (freight-in) is part of COGS. Shipping labels to send items to buyers are a separate business expense.

What if I didn't keep receipts for thrift store buys?

Use the best records you have, such as bank or card statements, and log the price on the day you buy from now on. The IRS expects records that support your cost.

Where does cost of goods sold go on my tax return?

Schedule C, Part III, lines 35 to 42. The total on line 42 is also entered on line 4.
