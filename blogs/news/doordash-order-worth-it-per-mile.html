Is a DoorDash Order Worth It? Pay Per Mile Math (2026, 76c/mile)

Short answer: divide what the order pays by the miles you drive for it, including the drive back if you have to go back. At the 2026 IRS rate of 76 cents a mile (July 1 onward), an order paying under about $1 a mile leaves you very little once the car is counted. A $7.50 order for 9 miles leaves 66 cents.

Why 76 cents

The IRS standard mileage rate is its estimate of what a business mile costs you: gas, wear, maintenance, insurance and depreciation together. For 2026 it is 72.5 cents a mile through June 30 and 76 cents from July 1. Your own car may cost more or less per mile, but it is never free, and the IRS number is the one most drivers use for their deduction.

The $1-a-mile rule

Many drivers only accept orders that pay at least $1 a mile. At 76 cents a mile that leaves 24 cents a mile. It is a floor, not a target: a $10 order for 10 miles leaves $2.40.

Log the miles - they are also your deduction

The same miles that eat into each order are a tax deduction if you can prove them. The IRS wants a written log made at or near the time: date, miles and business purpose. 10,000 business miles in 2026, split evenly before and after July 1, is $7,425 of deductions at the standard rates.
