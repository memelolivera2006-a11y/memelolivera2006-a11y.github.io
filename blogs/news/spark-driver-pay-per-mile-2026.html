Spark Driver Pay Per Mile 2026: Real Pay After Miles and Taxes

Short answer: Spark pays by the offer, not by the mile, and the drive back to the store is usually unpaid. So divide each offer's pay, tip included, by all the miles it costs you, there and back. At the 2026 IRS rate of 76 cents a mile, an offer that pays under about $1 a mile leaves almost nothing. In our examples, pay runs from $0.44 to $1.40 a mile, and the $22.00 offer with a big tip ends up one of the worst hours once the miles are counted.

How Spark pay works, in one paragraph

Per Walmart's Spark Driver FAQ, you earn for each offer you complete, and each offer shows an estimated amount before you accept it. Offers come in several types, including grocery and other store deliveries, returns, and Shopping & Delivery, where you shop the list in the store before you drive. Drivers keep 100% of customer-confirmed tips, and as independent contractors they can drive for other apps too. What the offer does not pay for is the drive back to the store or zone for the next one. Those miles cost exactly as much as the paid ones.

Step 2: the real cost of a mile, and the real hour

The IRS standard mileage rate is its estimate of the full cost of a business mile: gas, oil, repairs, tires, insurance, registration and depreciation. For 2026 it is 72.5 cents through June 30 and 76 cents from July 1. Below, each offer is charged 76 cents a mile, then self-employment tax (15.3% of 92.35% of profit) and 12% income tax on profit after half the self-employment tax.

Your own numbers will differ with your car, your store and your tips, which is the point of tracking them. If your car costs less to run than the IRS rate, your real hour is better than this; if it costs more, it is worse.

Why the store drive matters more on Spark

On restaurant apps, the next order is often near the last drop-off. Spark offers usually start at a store, so after a long delivery you often drive most of the way back empty. A rough rule: if an offer sends you more than about 8 miles out, double the miles before you judge the pay. If you live between two stores, an offer that ends near home or near the other store can cut the drive back.

The miles are also your biggest tax deduction

Say you drive 3 shifts a week for 40 weeks, about 90 miles a shift: 10,800 business miles. Logged, half before July and half after, that is a standard mileage deduction of $8,019.00. Self-employment tax alone on that much profit is about $1,133.05, before income tax. You keep that money only if you have a mileage log made at or near the time: date, miles and purpose. The unpaid drive back to the store counts as part of your working miles; the drive from home to your first store and back home at the end are where the commuting rules come in (IRS Topic 510), so log them as their own line.

Tips: count them, keep them separate

Tips are taxable income, and they are often the difference between a good and a bad offer. Log them apart from base pay: the new federal deduction for qualified tips (up to $25,000 a year, income tax only, not self-employment tax) needs the tip amounts, and app drivers may qualify. A 20-second check before you accept

  1. Round-trip miles: the offer's miles plus the drive back to the store.
  2. Divide the pay by those miles. Under $1.25 a mile, the car takes more than half at 76 cents.
  3. For shopping offers, divide by the hours. Count the time in the store and at checkout.

Pay, offer types and tips vary by store, city and time, and change often, so your own records are the only numbers that count.

FAQ

How much does Spark pay per mile?

There is no set per-mile rate: each offer shows an estimated total. Divide that total, tip included, by the round-trip miles, drive back to the store included, to get your own number.

Does Spark pay for mileage or gas?

Spark's FAQ describes pay per offer with an estimated amount shown up front; it does not list a separate mileage or gas payment. You cover the car, which is why logged miles are a tax deduction.

Do Spark drivers keep their tips?

Walmart's Spark FAQ says drivers keep 100% of customer-confirmed tips. Tips are taxable income.

Do Spark drivers pay taxes?

Yes. Spark drivers are independent contractors, so no tax is withheld. You owe self-employment tax on profit once it reaches $400 for the year, plus income tax, and may need quarterly estimated payments.
