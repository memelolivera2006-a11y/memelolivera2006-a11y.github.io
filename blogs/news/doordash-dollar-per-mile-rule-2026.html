Where the rule comes from

It is a driver rule of thumb, not an app policy. Neither DoorDash nor Uber Eats pays a set rate per mile: each offer shows a total for the order, and you divide that by the miles yourself. The dollar line got popular because it is easy to do in two seconds on a small screen. The problem is which miles you divide by and what a mile really costs.

Two ways to count a mile's cost

  • Gas and repairs only: what leaves your wallet this week. With gas at $3.20 and 25 mpg that is 12.8 cents, plus about 10 cents for oil, tires and repairs: roughly 23 cents a mile (example numbers).
  • Full cost: the IRS standard mileage rate, which adds insurance, registration and the car losing value with every mile. For 2026 it is 72.5 cents through June 30 and 76 cents from July 1. You pay those costs too, just later, when the car needs replacing.

The $1 rule looks fine under the first view and thin under the second. The honest answer sits closer to the second, because the car you wear out is the business's biggest cost.

A floor that holds up in 2026

  1. Count every mile. Restaurant, customer, and the drive back to a busy area. The miles on the offer screen leave out the return.
  2. $1.50 a mile minimum, $2 for long trips. Long trips usually mean an empty drive back.
  3. Estimate the time. If the offer can't clear about $20 an hour on screen, it won't clear a wage after the car and tax.
  4. Use your own car's numbers. A paid-off car that gets 40 mpg costs less per mile than the IRS rate; a financed SUV costs more. Track a month of real costs and adjust the floor.

Every mile you drive also lowers your tax

Logged business miles come off your profit at 76 cents each. A driver who logs 200 miles a week for 40 weeks deducts $6,080.00, which cuts about $859.08 of self-employment tax alone. Those miles only count if you write them down: date, miles and purpose.

FAQ

What is the $1 per mile rule for DoorDash?

A driver rule of thumb: decline any offer paying less than $1 for each mile it costs you. DoorDash does not pay per mile; you divide the offer total by the miles yourself.

Is $1 a mile good for DoorDash in 2026?

Usually not. The 2026 IRS rate puts a mile's full cost at 76 cents from July 1, so $1 a mile leaves 24 cents before tax. Many drivers use $1.50 to $2 a mile with the drive back counted.

Should I count the miles back home?

For deciding whether an offer is worth it, yes: you drive them either way. For taxes, the drive home after your last order may not be deductible depending on your setup, so log it and ask a tax professional.

Does the rule work for Uber Eats too?

The math is the same for any app that shows a total per offer: Uber Eats, Grubhub, Spark, Instacart. Divide by all the miles, then check the hour.
