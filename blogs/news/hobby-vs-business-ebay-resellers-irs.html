Hobby vs Business for eBay Flippers: IRS Rules, Worked Out

Short answer: if you buy things to resell, do it regularly and aim to make money, the IRS treats your eBay flipping as a business: profit goes on Schedule C, fees, shipping and supplies are deductible, and self-employment tax applies. If it is really a pastime with no profit motive, it is a hobby: the income still goes on your return (Schedule 1, line 8j), you can subtract what the items cost you, but none of the other expenses are deductible, and a loss can't reduce your other income. You don't get to pick the label; the facts decide it.

This is about items you bought to resell. Selling your own used stuff for less than you paid is a different case, covered in do you pay taxes on eBay and Poshmark sales.

The IRS test: nine factors

The IRS says to weigh all the facts, using nine factors, and that no single factor is more important than another. Here they are, with what each looks like for a reseller:

IRS factor What it looks like for a flipper
Run it in a businesslike way, with complete and accurate books and records A sale-by-sale log with cost, fees and shipping. A separate bank account or PayPal helps.
The time and effort you put in shows you intend to make a profit Regular sourcing trips, listing sessions, a schedule.
You depend on the income for your livelihood Not required, but it counts if true.
Losses are from things outside your control, or normal for a startup phase A first year of buying stock before it sells is normal.
You change methods to improve profitability Dropping categories that lose money, raising prices, switching platforms.
You or your advisers have the knowledge to run it as a business Knowing comps, fees and what sells.
You made a profit in similar activities before A past selling business counts.
The activity makes a profit in some years, and how much See the 3-of-5-years rule below.
You can expect a future profit from assets used in the activity going up in value Less common for flippers; more for collectors.

Look at the first factor again. Records are not just paperwork for tax time: keeping proper books is itself evidence that you are running a business.

The 3-out-of-5-years rule

Under section 183(d) of the tax code, if an activity makes a profit (income more than deductions) in 3 or more of 5 consecutive years, it is presumed to be for profit unless the IRS shows otherwise. Missing it does not make you a hobby; it just means the nine factors decide. New sellers can file Form 5213 to postpone that determination until the close of the fourth tax year after the year they started, so the first five years are judged together. Read the form's instructions before filing: the election has deadlines and side effects.

Why are hobby expenses not deductible at all? They count as miscellaneous itemized deductions, and section 67(h) allows none for any tax year beginning after 2017. IRS Publication 529 puts it plainly: hobby expenses "can no longer be deducted." Some older pages still describe a deduction up to hobby income; that rule is gone. Cost of goods sold is different: Treasury regulation 1.183-1(e) lets you figure a hobby's gross income as receipts minus the cost of the goods sold, if you do it consistently.

Worked example 1: a profitable year

A flipper sells $6,000 of items that cost $2,000, pays $800 in platform fees, $700 in shipping labels and $150 in supplies. Tax uses a 12% income tax bracket and ignores the QBI deduction to keep it simple.

As a business As a hobby
Taxable amount $2,350.00 profit $4,000.00 (sales minus item cost)
Self-employment tax $332.04 $0.00
Income tax at 12% $262.08 $480.00
Total $594.12 $480.00

Surprise: in a low bracket, the hobby label costs less here, $114.12 less, because self-employment tax outweighs the lost deductions. That is not a loophole. Worked example 2: a losing year

Same flipper, slower year: $3,000 of sales, $1,200 item cost, $400 fees, $350 labels, $120 supplies, and $1,200 for a storage unit. They also have a W-2 job.

As a business As a hobby
Result -$270.00 (a loss) $1,800.00 taxable
Self-employment tax $0.00 $0.00
Income tax at 12% -$32.40 (loss lowers tax on wages) $216.00
Total -$32.40 $216.00

As a hobby, this seller pays $216.00 of tax in a year they lost money. As a business, the loss lowers their tax by $32.40. Business status protects you in exactly the years you need it, and you only keep it with records that show a real profit motive.

How to look like the business you are

  • Log every item: date bought, what you paid, where, date sold, sale price, fees and shipping.
  • Keep business money apart: one account or PayPal for buying and payouts.
  • Review your numbers monthly and act on them: drop what loses, reprice what sits.
  • Count inventory at year end. Unsold items are not this year's deduction; see cost of goods sold for resellers.
  • If you expect to owe $1,000 or more for the year, make estimated payments; the next one is due January 15, 2027.

FAQ

Is selling on eBay a hobby or a business?

If you buy items to resell, regularly, to make money, it is generally a business. Selling your own used things occasionally is neither: losses aren't taxable and aren't deductible.

Can I deduct expenses if eBay is a hobby?

No. Hobby expenses are miscellaneous itemized deductions, and none are allowed for tax years after 2017. You can still subtract the cost of the goods you sold from your receipts.

Do I pay self-employment tax on hobby income?

No. Self-employment tax applies to net earnings from a business. Hobby income gets income tax only.

What is the 3 out of 5 years rule?

If an activity shows a profit in 3 or more of 5 consecutive years, it is presumed to be a for-profit business unless the IRS proves otherwise (26 U.S.C. 183(d)).
