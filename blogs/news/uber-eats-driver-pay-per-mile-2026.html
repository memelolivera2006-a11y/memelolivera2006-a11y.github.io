Uber Eats Driver Pay Per Mile 2026: Real Expense Math

Short answer: divide what an Uber Eats offer pays, tip included, by all the miles it costs you, including the drive back to a busy area. Then subtract the cost of those miles. At the 2026 IRS rate of 76 cents a mile, an offer needs to pay well over $1 a mile to leave real money. In our example dinner shift, $95.00 for four hours looks like $23.75 an hour and works out to about $8.94 an hour after the car and taxes.

What the offer screen tells you, and what it leaves out

In most US cities, Uber Eats shows an upfront offer before you accept: the estimated pay (often with the customer's tip included), the estimated time and the distance of the trip. Uber says couriers keep 100% of customer tips. What the screen does not show is the drive back after the drop-off, which can be several miles if the customer lives outside the busy zone. Those miles cost exactly as much as paid ones.

Your own cost per mile (the real expense math)

The IRS rate is a national average. To know what your car costs, add up a year of costs and divide by the miles you drive. Here is an example 25 mpg sedan driven 15,000 miles a year:

This car costs about 52 cents a mile to run, less than the IRS rate. A newer car with a loan, or one that needed a big repair this year, can cost more. Either way, gas is only about a quarter of the true cost, which is why judging offers by gas alone makes almost every order look good.

Tips: count them, and keep them separate

Tips are often the biggest part of a good offer, and they are taxable income. Starting with 2025 returns, the new deduction for qualified tips can cover tips earned in occupations that customarily receive them, and the IRS's April 2026 final rules include app-based delivery drivers. It reduces income tax only, not self-employment tax, is capped at $25,000 a year, and phases out at higher incomes. Logging tips separately from base pay is what lets you claim it.

Three rules that protect your hourly

  1. Count the drive back before you accept. If the drop-off is outside your zone, add those miles to the offer.
  2. Set a floor per mile, not per order. $1.50 a mile, all miles counted, leaves about 74 cents after the IRS rate.
  3. Log every mile you drive while working, including the unpaid ones. They are a deduction: 1,000 business miles after July 1, 2026 is $760 off your taxable profit.

Your own cost or the IRS rate: which one to use?

Use your own cost per mile to decide which offers to take and which apps to drive. It is what actually leaves your pocket. Use the IRS rate for your taxes if you choose the standard mileage method: you deduct 72.5 or 76 cents for every logged business mile no matter what your car really costs. For a car like our example, that is a nice gap: it costs about 52 cents a mile to run but earns a 76-cent deduction after July 1. If your car costs more than the IRS rate, for example a financed SUV, compare the actual expense method; see standard mileage vs actual expenses. One catch: to use the standard rate for a car at all, you generally have to use it in the first year that car is used for business.

Pay per mile or pay per hour?

Both, in that order. Pay per mile tells you whether the car is being paid for. Pay per hour, after the miles, tells you whether your time is. A short, slow order in a busy downtown can pay $2 a mile and still be a poor hour if you wait 15 minutes at the restaurant. A highway order can pay a decent hour and still lose money per mile. Track a few weeks of shifts and you will see which zones, times and order types do both.

FAQ

How much does Uber Eats pay per mile in 2026?

There is no single rate: pay varies by city, time and offer, and most offers show an upfront total rather than a per-mile price. Divide each offer's pay by all the miles it costs you to get your own number.

Do Uber Eats drivers keep their tips?

Uber says couriers keep 100% of customer tips. Tips are taxable income, but they may qualify for the new tips deduction, which lowers income tax (not self-employment tax) up to $25,000 a year.

Is gas the only cost I should subtract?

No. In our example car gas is about 12.8 cents of a 52-cent mile. Tires, repairs, insurance and depreciation are real costs too; they just arrive later.

Can I deduct the miles back to a busy area?

Miles driven while you are working between orders are generally business miles. Log them with the date, miles and purpose. IRS Topic 510 covers the commuting rules for the first and last drive of the day.
