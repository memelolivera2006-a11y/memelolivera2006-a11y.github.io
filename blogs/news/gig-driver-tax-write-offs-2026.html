What app drivers can usually deduct

  • Car costs, one of two ways: the standard mileage rate times business miles, or the business share of actual costs. Not both. See which method saves more.
  • Parking and tolls while working. Deductible on top of the standard rate.
  • Car-loan interest, the business share, if you are self-employed. Also allowed with the standard rate.
  • Phone and data plan, the share you use for the apps. A phone used 60% for driving is 60% deductible.
  • Supplies: insulated bags, phone mounts, chargers, dash cams, and shopping bags for grocery orders.
  • Platform fees you pay yourself, such as some background-check or subscription fees.
  • Half of your self-employment tax, taken on your Form 1040 automatically by tax software.
  • Self-employed health insurance, if you pay your own premiums and are not eligible for an employer plan.
  • Qualified business income deduction: up to 20% of your net profit for many drivers.

New for 2025-2028: tips

The "no tax on tips" deduction covers occupations that customarily received tips. The IRS final rules (April 2026) list taxi and rideshare drivers and goods delivery people, and say these include app-based drivers. Only tips you actually receive count, not any amount the platform keeps, and the deduction has a yearly cap and an income phase-out. For self-employed drivers it cannot be more than your net profit from driving. It reduces income tax, not self-employment tax, so keep tips separate from base pay in your records.

An example year

Write-off Basis Amount
Business miles (standard rate) 12,000 miles $8,910.00
Parking and tolls receipts $240.00
Phone and plan, 60% business $85/mo $612.00
Insulated bags, phone mount, chargers receipts $90.00
Total off your profit $9,852.00

If this driver earned $28,000 from the apps, these write-offs cut taxable profit by about a third, which also shrinks the 15.3% self-employment tax. That is why the log matters more than any single receipt.

What you usually cannot deduct

  • Traffic and parking tickets.
  • Meals while driving, and regular clothes.
  • Personal miles, and miles you cannot back up with a log.
  • Gas, repairs or insurance on top of the standard rate. The rate already covers them.

The drive from home to the first pickup is a gray area that depends on your setup. Ask a tax professional.
