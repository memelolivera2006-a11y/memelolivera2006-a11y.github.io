Delivery Apps Pay Per Mile Compared (2026): DoorDash, Uber Eats, Spark, Shipt and More

Short answer: none of the big delivery apps pays a fixed rate per mile in 2026. They all show one total per offer, and the number that matters is that total divided by every mile it costs you, empty miles included. With the car at the IRS rate of 76 cents a mile, an offer needs about $2 a mile or more to leave a real hourly wage after tax, whichever app sends it. These are honest numbers, not pessimistic ones: the car really costs about that much per mile over its life, you just pay it later in repairs, tires and a lower trade-in.

Which app pays the most per mile?

It depends on your market, your hours and which offers you take, not on the app's name. The fastest way to know is to log a few weeks on two apps side by side: pay, tips, all miles and minutes for every offer, then compare profit per mile and per hour. That one table usually settles it.

FAQ

Which delivery app pays the most per mile?

None pays a fixed per-mile rate, so it depends on your area and the offers you accept. Log pay and all miles for a few weeks on each app and compare profit per mile and per hour.

What is a good pay per mile for delivery?

Counting every mile, drive back included, about $2 a mile or more. At the 2026 IRS rate of 76 cents a mile, offers under $1.25 a mile leave little after the car and tax.

Do delivery apps pay for gas?

No app pays a separate gas allowance; distance only feeds into the offer total. Logged miles are your tax deduction at 76 cents a mile from July 1, 2026.
