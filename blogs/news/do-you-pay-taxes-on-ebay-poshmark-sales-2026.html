Do You Pay Taxes Selling on eBay or Poshmark? 2026 1099-K Rules

Short answer: it depends on what you are selling. Clearing out your own closet for less than you paid is not taxable. Buying things to flip is: that is business income, and you pay tax on the profit, not on what buyers paid. For 2026, eBay, Poshmark, Mercari and Depop only have to send a federal 1099-K once you pass $20,000 and 200 sales, but the tax applies either way.

Three kinds of selling, three answers

What you sold Taxable? Where it goes
Your own stuff, for less than you paid No, and the loss is not deductible Nothing, unless a 1099-K reports it (see below)
Your own stuff, for more than you paid Yes, the gain Form 8949 and Schedule D (capital gain)
Things you bought to resell Yes, the profit Schedule C, plus self-employment tax

Clearing out your closet

Item You paid Sold for Result
Winter jacket $120.00 $45.00 Loss, not taxable
Old phone $700.00 $210.00 Loss, not taxable
Vintage band tee $5.00 $60.00 Gain of $55, taxable

Most closet sales lose money, so there is nothing to tax. The one item that sold for more than it cost is a small capital gain. Keep a note of what you originally paid, even a rough receipt or order email, in case you ever need to show it.

Flipping: you pay on profit, so the records are the whole game

Say you made 260 sales in 2026 for $7,800.00. That is over 200 sales but under $20,000, so no federal 1099-K, but this is a business and it goes on Schedule C. What you subtract decides the bill:

Amount
Sales (what buyers paid you for items) $7,800.00
What you paid for the items − $1,950.00
Platform fees − $1,145.30
Shipping labels you paid − $1,248.00
Supplies (mailers, tape, printer labels) − $180.00
Profit $3,276.70
Taxed on Self-employment tax (15.3% of 92.35%)
With records $3,276.70 $462.98
No records, taxed on sales $7,800.00 $1,102.10
Difference $4,523.30 $639.12

That gap is self-employment tax alone, before income tax. Self-employment tax starts once your net profit reaches $400 for the year, far below any 1099-K threshold. The cost of your inventory is the deduction most flippers lose, because thrift and garage-sale buys rarely come with receipts. Log the price the day you buy.

If you get a 1099-K for personal items you sold at a loss

A 1099-K reports gross payments, and the IRS knows not all of it is income. For personal items sold at a loss, the IRS says to enter the 1099-K amount on Schedule 1 as other income (line 8z) and the same amount as an adjustment (line 24z), so it nets to zero. If the form mixes personal items and flips, split it: personal items as above, flips on Schedule C. Some states send 1099-Ks at lower amounts, so you may get one even under $20,000.

Hobby or business?

If you buy to resell regularly and aim to make money, the IRS treats it as a business. That is usually better for you: a business deducts inventory, fees, shipping and supplies. Hobby income is taxed with no deductions for its expenses.

What to do before April

  • Download each platform's yearly sales report (and any 1099-K) in January.
  • Total what you paid for everything you sold, plus fees, labels and supplies.
  • Keep personal items and flips separate.
  • If you expect to owe $1,000 or more, pay the fourth-quarter estimate by January 15, 2027.
