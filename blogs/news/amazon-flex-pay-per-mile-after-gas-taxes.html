Amazon Flex Pay Per Mile 2026: Real Earnings After Gas & Taxes

Short answer: an Amazon Flex block pays by the block, not by the mile, so the miles are where your pay leaks out. In our example blocks, pay works out to $0.79 to $1.38 a mile. Gas alone costs about 12.8 cents a mile in a 25 mpg car, but the true cost of a mile, counting wear, tires and depreciation, is closer to the 2026 IRS rate of 76 cents. After that and taxes, a $77.00 suburban block can leave about $3.88 an hour of real profit.

How Amazon Flex pay works, in one paragraph

You pick up a block: a set window of time, usually a few hours, at a set price you see before you accept. You drive to the delivery station, load the route and deliver it. The block price stays the same whether the route is 20 miles or 100. Whether tips are possible depends on the kind of offer, so check your earnings history before you count on them. Amazon does not pay for the drive to the station or home again.

That is why pay per mile is the number to watch on Flex. Two blocks that pay the same hourly rate can be very different jobs once you count the miles.

Step 1: pay per mile, before the car

Take the block pay and divide it by every mile the block makes you drive, including the round trip to the station. Here are four example blocks (example numbers, not Amazon's rates):

After gas, every block looks fine. That is the trap: gas is the cost you see at the pump, but it is only a fraction of what a mile really costs you.

Step 2: the real cost of a mile

The IRS standard mileage rate is its estimate of the full cost of a business mile: gas, oil, repairs, tires, insurance, registration and depreciation together. For 2026 it is 72.5 cents through June 30 and 76 cents from July 1. Your own car might cost a bit more or less, but a mile is never only its gas.

Flex routes are stop-and-go with lots of short hops, and your car is often full of packages. That is hard on brakes and tires, so if anything, a Flex mile tends to cost more than a highway mile, not less.

Step 3: profit and taxes per block

Flex drivers are independent contractors, so nobody withholds tax. On profit (pay minus the cost of the miles) you owe self-employment tax of 15.3% on 92.35% of it, plus income tax. The table uses 12% income tax, a common bracket for part-time drivers, on profit after half the self-employment tax. Hours include 30 minutes of unpaid driving to and from the station.

Cash kept is what lands in your account after gas and taxes. Real profit also takes out the wear the car took, which you pay later as tires, repairs and a lower resale price. The spread-out route puts the most cash in your pocket today and earns almost nothing once the wear is counted: you are being paid for the miles, then paying them back in tires and resale value. The short block with a far station barely covers the car. These are example numbers: put your own in.

Why the miles also save you tax

Here is the good news hiding in those miles. Say you drive the suburban block 4 times a week for 50 weeks: $15,400.00 of pay and 14,800 miles. If you log those miles, half before July and half after, the standard mileage deduction is $10,989.00.

Profit taxed Self-employment tax
With a mileage log $4,411.00 $623.25
No log $15,400.00 $2,175.95
Difference $10,989.00 $1,552.70

That is self-employment tax alone, before income tax. The deduction only holds up if you have a log made at or near the time: date, miles and purpose. The drive to the station is the tricky part: whether those miles are deductible depends on your setup (IRS Topic 510 has the rules), so log them separately and ask a tax professional. They cost you money either way, so they always belong in your pay-per-mile math.

A quick check before you grab a block

  1. Estimate the miles: station round trip plus a typical route from that station. Your own history is the best guide.
  2. Divide pay by miles. Under about $1.25 a mile, the car takes more than half the pay at 76 cents.
  3. Divide what is left by the hours, station drive included. Compare that with your other apps, not with the hourly rate Amazon shows.

Pay rules, block prices and tip options vary by city and change over time, so treat your own records as the truth, not any average you read online.

FAQ

Does Amazon Flex pay for mileage or gas?

No separate mileage or gas payment is built into a standard block; the block price is all you get. You cover gas and wear yourself, which is why the miles are a tax deduction if you log them.

What is a good pay per mile on Amazon Flex?

At the 2026 IRS rate of 76 cents, anything under about $1.00 a mile leaves very little. Many drivers look for $1.50 or more a mile, station drive included, but compare it with what your other apps pay per hour.

How much should a Flex driver set aside for taxes?

Self-employment tax is 15.3% of 92.35% of profit, plus income tax. Many drivers save 25-30% of profit, and pay it in quarterly estimates if they expect to owe $1,000 or more for the year.

Can I deduct the drive to the Amazon delivery station?

It depends on your situation, so the safe move is to log it as its own line and ask a tax professional. IRS Topic 510 explains the commuting rules.
