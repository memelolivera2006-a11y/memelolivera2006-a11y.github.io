Standard Mileage vs Actual Expenses for Uber & DoorDash (2026)

Short answer: for most Uber, Lyft, DoorDash and Instacart drivers the standard mileage rate wins. In 2026 it is 72.5 cents a mile through June 30 and 76 cents from July 1, and a cheap, efficient car rarely costs that much to run. Actual expenses win when the car is expensive, thirsty, or needed big repairs, and only if you kept every receipt.

The two methods in one paragraph each

Standard mileage: business miles times the IRS rate. That single number stands in for gas, oil, repairs, tires, insurance, registration and depreciation. You still deduct business parking and tolls on top, and if you are self-employed, the business share of car-loan interest.

Actual expenses: add up what the car really cost for the year (gas, insurance, repairs, tires, registration, lease payments or depreciation) and deduct the business share. If 75% of your miles were for the apps, you deduct 75% of the costs. You still need a mileage log to prove that 75%.

The pattern: a paid-off efficient car makes the standard rate look generous. Actual expenses pull ahead only when yearly costs are high per mile driven, as with a financed SUV with heavy depreciation or a truck with a big repair year.

The rule that locks you in

To use the standard rate for a car at all, you must use it in the first year you use that car for business. If you start with actual expenses and take accelerated depreciation, you cannot switch that car to the standard rate later. Going the other way is allowed: start with standard mileage and you can move to actual expenses in a later year, with straight-line depreciation. So if you are unsure in year one, the standard rate keeps both doors open.

What you need for either method

  • A mileage log made at or near the time: date, miles, and which app. Both methods need it.
  • Receipts for gas, repairs, insurance and so on, if you want the option to use actual expenses.
  • Total miles for the year (odometer on Jan 1 and Dec 31), to work out the business share.
