How Lyft pay works, in one paragraph

Each ride request shows an estimated pay before you accept. Since 2024 Lyft has promised drivers in the US that, each week, they earn at least 70% of what riders paid after external fees, and pays the difference if they fall short. It is checked over the week, not ride by ride, so a single ride can still pay you well under 70%. And the 70% is of the fare, before your own costs: gas, the car's wear, insurance and tax all come out of your share.

Example: riders pay $1,000.00 for your rides in a week, after external fees. The promise means Lyft pays you at least $700.00. If the rides added up to $640.00, Lyft adds $60.00. Either way, the miles are still yours to pay for.

Step 2: the real hour after the car and tax

The IRS standard mileage rate is its estimate of the full cost of driving a mile: gas, oil, repairs, tires, insurance, registration and depreciation. For 2026 it is 72.5 cents through June 30 and 76 cents from July 1. Each ride is charged 76 cents a mile, then self-employment tax (15.3% of 92.35% of profit) and 12% income tax on profit after half the self-employment tax.

A 20-second check before you accept

  1. Add the pickup miles to the ride miles, and the drive back if the drop-off is far from where rides come from.
  2. Divide the pay by all of them. Under $1.25 a mile, the car takes more than half at 76 cents.
  3. Skip long pickups for short rides unless the pay covers them.

Pickup miles are deductible too

Driving to a pickup, the ride itself and driving between rides are all business miles. Logged at 76 cents, 250 miles a week for 40 weeks is $7,600.00 off your profit. An app's yearly summary may not match the miles you can deduct, so keep your own log: date, miles and purpose, made at or near the time.

FAQ

How much does Lyft pay per mile in 2026?

There is no single per-mile rate you can rely on: each request shows an estimated total. Divide it, tip included, by the ride miles plus the pickup miles to get your real number.

What is Lyft's 70% guarantee?

Lyft promises that each week drivers earn at least 70% of rider payments after external fees, and pays the difference if they don't. It is measured weekly, not per ride, and it is before your car costs and taxes.

Does Lyft pay for the drive to the pickup?

Short pickups are generally not paid by the mile; some long pickups can earn extra, and the request shows the pickup distance before you accept. Either way, count those miles when you judge the ride: they cost the same as ride miles, and they are deductible if you log them.

Do Lyft drivers pay taxes?

Yes. Lyft drivers are independent contractors, so no tax is withheld. You owe self-employment tax once profit reaches $400 for the year, plus income tax, and may need quarterly estimated payments.
