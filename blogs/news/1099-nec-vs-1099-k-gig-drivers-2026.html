1099-NEC vs 1099-K for DoorDash, Uber & Instacart Drivers (2026)

Short answer: for 2026 pay, an app generally has to send you a 1099-NEC once it pays you $2,000 or more for your services (up from $600), and a 1099-K only when payments top $20,000 and 200 transactions. Many part-time drivers will get no form at all. That does not change what you owe: all of it is taxable income, form or no form.

1099-NEC vs 1099-K in one line each

  • 1099-NEC (nonemployee compensation): what a company paid you directly for work. Threshold for payments made in 2026: $2,000, adjusted for inflation from 2027.
  • 1099-K (payment card and third-party network transactions): what a payment platform processed for you. Federal threshold: more than $20,000 and more than 200 transactions in the year. Both have to be true.

Which one you get depends on how the app pays you, and some drivers get both from the same app, for example a 1099-K for rides and a 1099-NEC for bonuses or referrals. Check the tax section of each app's driver dashboard in January.

Example: $15,000 across two apps, zero forms

App Reports pay on 2026 pay Transactions Form sent?
Delivery app A 1099-NEC $1,800.00 310 No: under $2,000
Rideshare app B 1099-K $13,200.00 640 No: 200+ transactions but under $20,000

This driver made $15,000.00 and, under the federal thresholds, gets no 1099 from either app. They still report all of it on Schedule C. Some states use lower 1099-K thresholds, so you may get a form anyway.

Why the mileage log matters more than the form

Say the same driver drove 9,000 business miles, half before July and half after. At 72.5 and 76 cents that is $6,682.50 off, leaving $8,317.50 of profit.

No log, no deduction $15,000.00 $2,119.43
Difference $6,682.50 $944.20

That gap is self-employment tax alone, before income tax. Self-employment tax kicks in once your net earnings reach $400, which is far below any 1099 threshold.

If a 1099-K shows more than you were paid

A 1099-K reports gross payments, which can include amounts the platform kept as fees or passed to someone else. Report the gross on Schedule C, then deduct the platform's fees as an expense so your profit matches reality. The app's yearly tax summary usually breaks this down. If the form is simply wrong, ask the platform for a corrected one.

What to do in January

  • Download every 1099 and the yearly tax summary from each app, even if a form never arrives by mail.
  • Add up pay and tips per app from your own records and compare.
  • Total your business miles by half-year (72.5 cents before July 1, 76 cents after).
  • Pay the fourth-quarter estimate by January 15, 2027 if you owe.
