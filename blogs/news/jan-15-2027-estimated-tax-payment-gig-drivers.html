Jan 15 2027 Estimated Tax Payment for Gig Drivers: Step by Step

Short answer: the fourth and last estimated tax payment for 2026 is due Friday, January 15, 2027. It covers September through December. To size it: total your 2026 profit, work out self-employment tax and income tax, take the smaller of 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's, and pay whatever you have not paid yet. You can skip it only if you file your 2026 return by February 1, 2027 and pay the whole balance with it.

Who has to make the January payment

Per the 2026 Form 1040-ES, you generally must pay estimated tax if you expect to owe at least $1,000 for 2026 after withholding and credits, and your withholding and credits will be less than the smaller of 90% of your 2026 tax or 100% of your 2025 tax (110% if your 2025 adjusted gross income was over $150,000). App payouts have no withholding, so most full-time and many part-time drivers are in.

If you skipped the April, June or September payments, paying in January does not erase the penalty for those quarters, but it stops it from growing and is still worth doing.

Step 1: total your 2026 profit

Add up pay and tips from every app for January through December. Use your own records, not just the 1099s: for 2026 pay, a 1099-NEC is generally only required at $2,000 and a 1099-K above $20,000 and 200 transactions, so many drivers will not get a form for every app. December is not over when you pay, so estimate the rest of the month from your recent weeks.

Step 2: subtract your expenses

For most drivers the big one is miles: 72.5 cents a mile for January-June 2026 and 76 cents from July 1. Split your logged business miles by half-year. Add parking, tolls, the business share of your phone, and supplies.

Step 3: self-employment tax

Self-employment tax is 15.3% of 92.35% of your net profit (12.4% Social Security, 2.9% Medicare). The Social Security part stops at $184,500 of combined wages and self-employment earnings for 2026, which most drivers never reach. It applies once net earnings reach $400.

Step 4: income tax

Subtract half of your self-employment tax and your deduction (the 2026 standard deduction is $16,100 for single filers), then apply the 2026 brackets: 10% up to $12,400 of taxable income and 12% up to $50,400 for single filers. Add any other income, such as a W-2 job, before you do this. The qualified business income deduction can lower income tax further; leaving it out keeps your estimate on the safe side.

Notice where the tax is: almost all of it is self-employment tax. The standard deduction wipes out nearly all income tax at this profit level, but nothing reduces self-employment tax except business expenses. That is why the mileage log is the most valuable record a driver keeps.

Step 5: check the safe harbor and size the payment

You avoid the underpayment penalty if, by each due date, you have paid a quarter of the smaller of 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's. Our driver paid $500 in April, June and September, and owed $1,900 on the 2025 return.

Paying $400.00 by January 15 meets the safe harbor. The remaining $644.09 can wait until the return is filed, with no underpayment penalty. Paying the full rest now is also fine; it just means less to pay in April. These are example numbers: put your own in.

Step 6: pay it

  • IRS Direct Pay: free bank transfer, no sign-up. Choose estimated tax, Form 1040-ES, tax year 2026. Not 2027: picking the wrong year is a common January mistake.
  • Your IRS Online Account: pay and see every payment you have made for the year.
  • EFTPS: free, but you must enroll first, which takes time.
  • Debit or credit card: through an IRS-approved processor, for a fee.
  • Check with the 2026 Form 1040-ES voucher. Paying online avoids any question about the postmark date.

Keep the confirmation number. You will enter all four 2026 payments on your return in the spring. Your state may want its own estimated payment around the same date; check your state tax agency.

Two ways to skip or shrink it

  • File early: file your 2026 return by February 1, 2027 and pay the full balance, and you do not have to make the January 15 payment. This needs all your forms early, so it suits few drivers.
  • Have a W-2 job too? Raise your withholding on your last paychecks. Withholding is generally treated as paid evenly through the year, so extra withholding in December can cover earlier quarters as well.

If your driving income was very uneven during the year, the annualized income installment method (Form 2210, Schedule AI) may lower or remove a penalty for earlier quarters. Pub. 505 explains it.

FAQ

When is the fourth quarter estimated tax payment for 2026 due?

January 15, 2027. It is a Friday, so there is no weekend shift.

Do I have to pay on January 15 if I file early?

No, if you file your 2026 return by February 1, 2027 and pay the entire balance due with it.

What if I missed the earlier 2026 payments?

Pay what you can by January 15. The penalty is figured quarter by quarter, so it keeps growing until you pay. Paying now limits it.

Which tax year do I pick when paying in January 2027?

Tax year 2026. A payment marked 2027 is credited to next year's taxes instead.
