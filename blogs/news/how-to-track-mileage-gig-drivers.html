How to Track Mileage for Uber, DoorDash and Instacart (IRS Rules)

Short answer: for each business drive, write down the date, the miles, and the business purpose, at or near the time you drive. That record is what lets you deduct 72.5 cents a mile (January-June 2026) or 76 cents a mile (from July 1, 2026).

Also note your odometer on January 1 and December 31. It is not required for every trip, but it shows your total driving and backs up your business share.

Which miles count

  • Count: driving to pickups, deliveries and drop-offs, and driving between orders while you are working the app.
  • Usually not: commuting from home to a regular workplace. How the drive from home to your first order is treated depends on your situation - IRS Topic 510 explains the rules.
  • Personal stops in the middle of a shift are personal miles.

Why the app's number may not be enough

The tax summaries the apps send do not all measure the same thing. Some count only the miles while you are on an active trip or delivery, not the drive to your first pickup or between orders. Check what yours counts. Miles you drove for work but did not record are a deduction you lose - at 76 cents a mile, 2,000 missing miles is $1,520.

Make it a 30-second habit

  1. At the end of each shift, read the trip meter or your phone's mileage tracker.
  2. Type one row: date, app, miles, earnings.
  3. Once a month, check the total against your odometer.
