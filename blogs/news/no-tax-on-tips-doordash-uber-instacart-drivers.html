No Tax on Tips for DoorDash, Uber & Instacart Drivers (2026 Math)

Short answer: yes, app-based delivery and rideshare drivers are on the IRS list of occupations that can take the new no tax on tips deduction, for 2025 through 2028. You can deduct up to $25,000 of tips per return, but as a self-employed driver never more than your net profit from driving. It lowers your income tax only. Your self-employment tax stays the same.

Who qualifies

The deduction only covers tips earned in an occupation on the IRS list of jobs that customarily received tips before 2025. Two entries cover gig drivers:

  • Taxi and rideshare drivers and chauffeurs, which names app-based rideshare drivers (Uber, Lyft).
  • Goods delivery people, which names app or platform-based delivery (DoorDash, Uber Eats, Instacart, grocery and package delivery).

The rules in one list

  • Years: tax years 2025, 2026, 2027 and 2028.
  • Cap: $25,000 per return. Married couples share one $25,000, it is not per spouse.
  • Self-employed limit: the deduction cannot be more than your net income from the work the tips came from, figured before this deduction.
  • Income limit: it phases out once modified AGI passes $150,000 ($300,000 married filing jointly).
  • Filing: married drivers must file jointly, and the person who got the tips needs a Social Security number valid for work.
  • Where it goes: Schedule 1-A, Part II, which flows to Form 1040 line 13b. You do not have to itemize.
  • Proof: tips must be on a 1099 or other statement from the app, or reported by you. Keep each app's yearly summary showing tips separately from base pay.

Income tax saved$600.00 in the 12% bracket, $500.00 in the 10% bracket

The deduction comes off after your Schedule C profit is worked out, and self-employment tax is figured from that profit. That is why the tips deduction does not touch the $1,494.20.

When your profit caps the deduction

Same $5,000 of tips, but $12,000 total pay and $8,500 of mileage and expenses. Profit is $3,500, so the deduction is $3,500, not $5,000. Drivers who log every mile hit this cap more often. The miles still win: each mileage dollar cuts both self-employment tax and income tax, while the tips deduction only cuts income tax.

What to keep from now on

  • Tips and base pay as separate numbers, per app, per month.
  • Each app's yearly tax summary from the tax section of its driver dashboard.
  • A mileage log with dates, since the 2026 rate changes on July 1.
